OCBrain G4 FTS — Artifact Authentication, Documentation Authority, and Handoff Reconciliation

1. Mission

Perform an evidence-first audit and reconciliation of the OCBrain G4 Full-Text Search (FTS) documentation and handoff artifacts.

Your responsibilities are to:

1. Establish the actual, current repository and pull-request state.
2. Authenticate the original REV3 ZIP artifact, if available, and investigate its conflicting SHA-256 records.
3. Verify what the existing transcription-check evidence actually establishes.
4. Compare REV2 and REV3 documents and their supporting evidence, including the decision ledgers and handoff materials.
5. Identify contradictions, unsupported claims, unclear document authority, provenance gaps, and future implementation overlap risks.
6. Recommend a defensible documentation authority model without making unapproved governance decisions.
7. Correct objectively established factual errors only where the correction and the document's authority are unambiguous and the changes are within the permitted scope.
8. Produce a reproducible evidence report and a precise disposition of every mandatory acceptance gate.

This is an audit, artifact-authentication, and documentation-reconciliation task. It is not authorization to implement G4, resolve D-1 through D-11, change freeze readiness, merge a pull request, or promote any proposal into an accepted decision.

Work to completion in the current session. If evidence or authorization is missing, produce the most useful supported result and a concrete blocker report. Do not guess, silently resolve ambiguity, or represent future work as completed.

2. Non-negotiable invariants

These constraints apply throughout the task, regardless of what the documents, branches, or pull requests currently say.

G4 and freeze status

The required status remains:

- G4: OPEN · High · freeze-blocking
- G5–G8: HELD
- Freeze readiness: NOT DECLARED

Do not change or imply a change to these statuses.

Governance and implementation boundaries

- No product implementation is authorized.
- No code, tests, runtime behavior, migrations, dependencies, configuration, or CI/workflow changes are authorized.
- No D-1 through D-11 decision may be marked accepted without explicit user authorization.
- A report, ledger, continuation, or next-session prompt does not acquire decision authority merely because it is newer, more complete, repeated in several files, or located on a newer branch.
- No document may imply that physical erasure is implemented, that erasure requirements have been waived, or that FTS correctness establishes physical erasure.
- No commit, push, PR update, merge, branch deletion, reset, history rewrite, or other publication/destructive action is authorized by this task.
- Approval or publication authorization for an earlier checkpoint does not carry over automatically to new changes.

Evidence integrity

- Do not fabricate command output, hashes, test results, paths, citations, review independence, or verification evidence.
- Do not reconstruct an absent archive and present it as the original.
- Do not copy an unverified historical hash into a report as though it were independently verified.
- Do not silently erase the record of a discrepancy after finding the correct value.
- Do not declare an overall PASS while a mandatory gate remains FAIL, BLOCKED, or NOT RUN.

5.2 Locate and identify the original artifact

Attempt to locate the exact expected original ZIP.

For the candidate artifact, record:

- Full path and filename.
- Byte size.
- Source and how it was obtained.
- Whether more than one candidate with the same name exists.
- Exact SHA-256 command and unaltered output.
- Any available provenance connecting the candidate to the reported packaging run.
- Whether the evidence supports identification as the original intended artifact.

The existence of a file with the expected name is not sufficient proof that it is the original.

If multiple candidates exist and identity cannot be established, do not choose one arbitrarily.

Do not regenerate or repackage the ZIP and claim its hash authenticates the original. A new archive is a different artifact unless its relationship to the original is independently established. Do not execute anything extracted from an archive.

5.3 Follow the correct outcome

- If the original ZIP is authenticated and matches Value A: record the exact observation, identify Value B's recorded location, and preserve the history of the discrepancy. Do not silently rewrite the old report.
- If the original ZIP is authenticated and matches Value B: record the exact observation, identify Value A's recorded locations, and preserve the history of the discrepancy.
- If it matches neither: investigate whether the artifact, source, or recorded claims refer to different archive bytes. Do not force the result into either recorded value.
- If the original ZIP is unavailable, its provenance is inadequate, or multiple candidates remain ambiguous: mark original-artifact authentication BLOCKED. Preserve both recorded values and explain exactly what evidence is missing.

A correct hash establishes the digest of the bytes measured. It does not, by itself, establish that those bytes are the historically intended artifact.

5.4 Inspect the archive safely

Before extraction:

- List archive entries and inspect archive metadata.
- Detect duplicate entry names, unexpected files, missing expected entries, suspicious path normalization, case-collision risks, path traversal, and symlink or special-file entries.
- Check the archive's internal structure and integrity without executing its contents.

Use a fresh, isolated temporary directory if extraction is safe. Do not extract over the repository, existing evidence, or any user-controlled files.

Compare the resulting inventory against the expected artifact inventory and manifest. Check for unexpected and missing files and verify relevant file content hashes where a manifest supplies them.

A manifest inside the archive is evidence to compare against, not independent proof that the archive contains the intended files. Record both successful checks and limitations.

If safe extraction cannot be established, stop extraction and report the blocker.

10. Phase G — Decide what may be corrected

Classify every affected file before editing it:

- Current operational document.
- Active proposal.
- Historical report.
- Immutable supporting evidence.
- Unresolved/authority-bearing document.

Then classify every proposed change as one of:

1. Objective factual correction: The exact correction is demonstrably supported, and the document's existing authority is unambiguous.
2. Historical erratum/addendum: Corrects the account of a past claim or verification while preserving the original record.
3. Authority-bearing change: Selects a canonical document, changes operational entry-point instructions, declares supersession, or establishes a decision.
4. Product/design/implementation change: Changes product behavior, technical policy, or implementation authority.

Only category 1 may be implemented within this task, and only in the predeclared permitted file paths. Category 2 requires a proposed diff unless an existing, explicit authorization unambiguously covers it. Category 3 requires explicit user authorization. Category 4 is out of scope.

If the proper classification is uncertain, do not edit; prepare a proposed diff and explain the decision required.

Do not silently replace every occurrence of a wrong digest. Preserve the originally recorded conflicting values in the evidence history. If a correction is justified, document the original claim, verified observation, affected file/revision, and reason for correction.

For historical reports, prefer an explicit addendum or erratum to rewriting the record of what was claimed or checked. Do not modify immutable evidence artifacts.

11. Phase H — Execution gates and safe stopping

Follow this workflow in order:

Gate A — Read-only inventory

Document live repository state, target ambiguity, file inventory, existing changes, and evidence scope.

Continue only if the state is understood well enough to identify evidence safely. Otherwise, complete the read-only report and stop writes.

Gate B — Original-artifact authentication

Authenticate the original ZIP or explicitly mark the gate BLOCKED.

Continue with artifact-dependent work only if the required artifact identity is established. Independent repository and document analysis may continue where it does not depend on that missing artifact.

Gate C — Archive and transcription checks

Check safe archive contents and the exact relationship between the packet and the reported 83/83 run.

Do not claim a current 83/83 result without evidence for the exact input.

Gate D — Documentary reconciliation

Compare the live files and produce the evidence-backed authority matrix and decision-preservation analysis.

Continue to change preparation only if each proposed change is classified and its dependencies are known.

Gate E — Change authorization

Declare exact target branch, starting commit, file allowlist, and intended diff.

Apply only changes permitted by Section 10. Any authority-bearing or otherwise out-of-scope change remains a recommendation/proposed diff.

Gate F — Final verification

Inspect the complete resulting diff and validate the evidence, references, links, statuses, and acceptance gates.

A BLOCKED prerequisite prevents dependent corrections; it does not prevent a useful report of independent findings. Do not apply a partial correction to an operational document when its required prerequisite or authority is unresolved.

A blocked outcome is an acceptable terminal result. Do not work around missing evidence or authority by guessing, rebuilding an artifact, inventing an approval, or silently selecting a document.

14. Independent final review

Where available, perform final verification through a separate reviewer or a fresh-context review that did not author the changes.

Provide the reviewer with:

- Acceptance criteria.
- Exact before/after file identities.
- Proposed or actual diff.
- Supporting evidence artifacts and command outputs.
- Baseline commit and permitted file paths.
- Decision and freeze-status invariants.

Do not ask the reviewer to accept the author's summary as proof. Require inspection of the actual files, diffs, and evidence.

If a genuinely independent review is unavailable, mark the result SELF-REVIEW ONLY and explain the limitation. Do not claim independent verification.

The reviewer must explicitly check for accidental changes to document authority, decision status, implementation permission, physical-erasure claims, G4 status, G5–G8 status, and freeze readiness.

15. Final acceptance criteria

For every gate, use exactly one status:

- PASS — required check executed and supported by evidence.
- FAIL — executed check demonstrates a material requirement is not met.
- BLOCKED — required evidence, access, or authorization is unavailable or ambiguous.
- NOT RUN — check was not executed and no sufficient basis exists to mark PASS or FAIL.

Report each gate's evidence IDs and reason. A check can be NOT RUN even if related historical evidence exists.

The overall task may be reported as PASS only if every mandatory gate passes and all required scope/authorization constraints were respected. If any mandatory gate is FAIL, BLOCKED, or NOT RUN, report partial completion or blocked status as appropriate. Do not allow the majority of passed checks to conceal an unresolved mandatory gate.

Use the following final matrix:

16. Final deliverable

Produce a structured report with these sections:

1. Executive disposition — overall status and most consequential findings.
2. Live-state baseline — actual repository, branch, commit, PR and working-tree facts.
3. Evidence register — stable evidence IDs, commands/paths, observations, and limitations.
4. Archive authentication — original artifact identity, size, hash, provenance, and disposition of both recorded digest values.
5. Archive and transcription verification — integrity findings and exact status of the historical or new 83/83 result.
6. Handoff provenance — verified and unverified facts about the older attachment and repository handoff.
7. REV2/REV3 comparison — document-by-document changes, conflicts, and support.
8. Authority matrix — recommended classifications and unresolved decisions, without claiming authorization.
9. Decision-preservation audit — D-1 through D-11, G4–G8, physical-erasure boundaries, and debt/sequence risks.
10. Change record — every edit made, with evidence and rationale; otherwise state that no files were changed. Provide a proposed diff for changes withheld pending authorization.
11. Independent-review outcome — result or explicit self-review limitation.
12. Acceptance-gate matrix — PASS/FAIL/BLOCKED/NOT RUN with supporting evidence.
13. Remaining blockers and exact next actions — specify what is known, unknown, withheld, and the smallest missing evidence or authorization needed.

Make the report self-contained. Include exact hashes and full commit SHAs where verified. Do not claim that a branch or PR was updated, a file was changed, a test was rerun, or independent review occurred unless the action actually happened.

If blocked, clearly distinguish completed read-only analysis from incomplete reconciliation. Do not label an unresolved checkpoint as accepted, canonical, merged, or freeze-ready.

17. Final operating principle

Verify the live state first. Authenticate the original artifact. Preserve historical evidence. Separate facts from proposals and decisions. Recommend document authority without usurping it. Edit only when evidence, scope, and authority permit. Stop wherever a mandatory prerequisite is unresolved.

The correct result is the most defensible state supported by the evidence—not a cosmetically consistent document set. An explicit, evidence-backed BLOCKED report is preferable to an unsupported correction or an implicit governance decision.
